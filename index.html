The actual problem

Every procurement team I read about faces the same Monday morning situation. They export PO data from SAP, Oracle, or whatever ERP they run. The file has inconsistent supplier names, uncategorised rows, and no strategic structure. Someone spends three days cleaning it. By Thursday they have a Pareto chart. Leadership gets a slide that says "we should consolidate suppliers." Nothing changes.

The tools that solve this properly — Coupa, SAP Ariba, Suplari — cost anywhere from $30,000 to $500,000 a year and take months to implement. A 50-person manufacturer or a mid-size distributor cannot justify that. They stay in Excel.

SpendLens is not trying to replace those platforms. It is the analysis layer that any team can use today, before they have the budget for enterprise software — or to check the work of the software they already have.

How it works
01
Upload your PO export

Export purchase orders from SAP, Oracle, or any ERP as a CSV. You only need two columns to start: supplier name and amount. SpendLens handles the rest. There is no login, no integration, no IT ticket.

Any ERP · 2 columns minimum
02
Automatic classification

Every line item gets classified against the UNSPSC taxonomy — the same standard that SAP Ariba and Oracle use globally. Suppliers get segmented into Class A, B, and C using the Pareto method from Monczka and Handfield's textbook.

UNSPSC v24 · Pareto ABC
03
Kraljic positioning

Each spend category gets placed on the Kraljic Matrix automatically — Strategic, Leverage, Bottleneck, or Non-critical. This is the framework every CPO has read. SpendLens is the first free tool that does this from raw PO data.

Kraljic 1983 · HBR
04
Concentration ratio

For every category, the tool calculates CR-3: the share of spend captured by the top 3 suppliers. When CR-3 drops below 70%, the category is fragmented — you have too many suppliers and no leverage. That is an immediate consolidation opportunity.

CR-n · Industrial Organisation
05
Spend under management

If your data includes a preferred supplier flag, SpendLens calculates what percentage of your spend is going through contracted suppliers. The Hackett Group's world-class benchmark is 80%. Most organisations are at 50–65%.

SUM · Hackett Group 2024
06
Prioritised actions

The tool generates a ranked list of sourcing actions — consolidate tail spend here, run a competitive RFQ there, qualify an alternate supplier for that bottleneck category. Each action references the framework behind it and the SAP Ariba module that executes it.

Monczka et al. · SAP Ariba

The five outputs
1

Pareto / ABC Segmentation

Splits suppliers into three classes — A (top 20% of suppliers accounting for 80% of spend), B (next 30%, 15% of spend), C (tail spend). The Pareto chart shows where your money actually goes.

Monczka, Handfield et al. (2019) Purchasing and Supply Chain Management, 7th Ed., Ch.6
2

UNSPSC Taxonomy Classification

Every line item description gets classified into UNSPSC Level-1 Segments — the global standard used by SAP Ariba, Oracle, and the UN. No manual tagging required.

UNSPSC v24.0301 — United Nations Development Programme / GS1
3

Supplier Concentration Ratio (CR-3)

For each category, the tool calculates what percentage of spend goes to the top 3 suppliers. Below 70% means fragmentation — you are splitting spend across too many vendors and getting no price leverage from any of them.

Standard industrial organisation economics — applied to procurement portfolio management
4

Kraljic Portfolio Matrix

Every spend category is automatically placed in one of four quadrants: Strategic, Leverage, Bottleneck, or Non-critical. Each quadrant comes with a recommended sourcing strategy. This is the analysis that used to take most of the three days.

Kraljic, P. (1983). Purchasing must become supply management. Harvard Business Review, 61(5), 109–117.
5

Spend Under Management Ratio

If your data has a preferred supplier flag, SpendLens tells you what percentage of spend flows through contracted, approved vendors — and how far you are from the 80% world-class benchmark. The unmanaged portion is your maverick spend problem.

Hackett Group (2024) Procurement Key Issues Study — 80% SUM = top quartile procurement function
